# Grading rubric — Biomimicry Challenge Report (ZHAW Blockweek Bionics 2026)

Extracted verbatim from `Report_template_26_V3_final.docx`. The report is 70 % of the
final course grade. "The rubric emphasizes the quality of the bio-inspired transfer and
the engineering development of the concept. A visually attractive idea alone is not
sufficient for a high grade."

Each criterion is rated 1–4 and multiplied by its weight.

---

## 1. Challenge, Scoping & Functional Analysis — 15 %

| Pts | Descriptor |
|-----|------------|
| 4 Excellent | Problem and design question are precise; system boundaries, context and constraints are clear; functions are well formulated; functional diagram is insightful; 4–7 meaningful performance criteria are defined. |
| 3 Good | Clear problem and functions; system/context mostly clear; functional diagram and performance criteria are useful but show minor gaps. |
| 2 Satisfactory | Basic problem framing; functions or system boundaries partly unclear; functional diagram superficial; performance criteria generic. |
| 1 Needs Improvement | Problem framing unclear; important functions/context missing; no meaningful functional diagram or performance criteria. |

## 2. Biological Discovery & Abstraction — 20 %

| Pts | Descriptor |
|-----|------------|
| 4 | Strong research path; relevant natural models compared and selection/deselection justified; mechanisms explained in depth with scientific evidence and figures; convincing abstraction from biology to transferable principles. |
| 3 | Good natural models and mostly correct mechanisms; research path and abstraction clear but less detailed or critical. |
| 2 | Limited research depth; mechanisms remain descriptive; selection logic or abstraction is weak. |
| 1 | Poor or missing biological models; mechanisms unsupported or misunderstood; no meaningful abstraction. |

## 3. Engineering Concept Development — 25 %

| Pts | Descriptor |
|-----|------------|
| 4 | Clear engineering logic and Engineering Canvas; biological principles are convincingly translated into technical principles; at least two technically distinct, well-developed concepts with annotated engineering sketches; components, flows, materials and design decisions are explained. |
| 3 | Engineering logic is mostly clear; two meaningful concepts are developed and annotated; biological-to-technical transfer is understandable but some engineering aspects remain underdeveloped. |
| 2 | Concepts remain largely at idea/sketch level; functional logic or biological-to-technical transfer only partly explained; limited technical detail. |
| 1 | Concepts are superficial or primarily visual; little engineering development; biological inspiration is copied literally or technical transfer is unclear. |

## 4. Engineering Validation & Iteration — 15 %

| Pts | Descriptor |
|-----|------------|
| 4 | 2–3 critical engineering questions are derived from the concept; relevant assumptions are tested with appropriate calculations, estimates, experiments, models or other evidence; results influence concept selection and lead to a clearly documented improved Concept V2. |
| 3 | Critical questions and relevant checks are present; results are interpreted and some concept improvement is demonstrated. |
| 2 | Some technical checking is attempted, but questions are generic or calculations/evidence have little influence on the design. |
| 1 | No meaningful engineering questions or validation; functionality claims remain unsupported; no iteration demonstrated. |

## 5. Sustainability & Life's Principles — 10 %

| Pts | Descriptor |
|-----|------------|
| 4 | Relevant Life's Principles are applied critically; environmental benefits, limitations and trade-offs are discussed; circularity/resource aspects are integrated where relevant and linked to design decisions. |
| 3 | Relevant sustainability aspects and Life's Principles are discussed with reasonable connection to the concept. |
| 2 | Sustainability discussion is generic; Life's Principles are listed rather than applied; trade-offs receive little attention. |
| 1 | Sustainability claims are unsupported or largely absent; no meaningful application of Life's Principles. |

## 6. Scientific Quality & Critical AI Use — 10 %

| Pts | Descriptor |
|-----|------------|
| 4 | High-quality and varied sources; consistent citations; biological and technical claims are evidence-based; AI use is transparent and critical; important AI outputs, calculations and claims are independently checked. |
| 3 | Adequate sources and mostly consistent citations; AI use declared; most important claims are verified. |
| 2 | Weak or repetitive sources; inconsistent citations; AI use insufficiently documented or outputs accepted with limited verification. |
| 1 | Major citation problems; unsupported claims/plagiarism; AI-generated content used uncritically or not declared. |

## 7. Communication & Report Quality — 5 %

| Pts | Descriptor |
|-----|------------|
| 4 | Professional, concise and coherent report; figures, diagrams, calculations and tables are well integrated and readable; reasoning chain is easy to follow. |
| 3 | Clear report with useful visuals and generally coherent structure; minor presentation weaknesses. |
| 2 | Some unclear writing or inconsistent structure; visuals are weakly integrated or difficult to interpret. |
| 1 | Hard to follow; poor formatting; key figures/diagrams missing or unclear. |

conservative). Report the unrounded score as well.

## Formal requirements (template "Remarks" + Structure & Content)

- All chapters must be addressed; emphasis may differ by challenge.
- Guideline length 15–25 pages excluding references and appendices. No fixed length.
- **Use the headings from this template** — do not rename sections.
- Refer to all sources used, including AI-supported tools. AI output is not evidence;
  relevant biological and technical claims must be verified.
- ~20 references as a guideline; quality and relevance matter more than the number.
  One consistent citation style throughout.
- Appendix A: AI use statement — tool / used for / how it influenced the project / how
  the output was verified.

## Per-section content checklist (template "Structure & Content")

**1 Introduction** — background, relevance and aim stated clearly; design challenge and
intended contribution briefly introduced.

**2 Challenge & Scoping** — precise problem definition and clear design question;
context conditions and constraints; system of interest, subsystems, supersystem and
boundaries; key functions written as verbs, aligned with the taxonomy explainer; first
abstract, solution-neutral functional diagram with energy/material/information flows;
biologized questions; 4–7 performance criteria / KPIs including Life's Principles.

**3.1 Discovering & Abstracting** — AskNature / AskNature Chat, scientific literature and
other credible sources; explicit research path (how models were found, which selected,
which de-selected and why); biological strategies and mechanisms explained in depth (how
they work, not just the organism); figures or drawings; abstraction into a general
transferable principle with biological observation, abstraction and technical
interpretation clearly distinguished; relevant Life's Principles identified.

**3.2 Creating** — structured ideation (Crazy 8s and/or Morphological Matrix) with at
least three initial concept combinations before selecting two; at least two technically
distinct concept variants with annotated drawings showing components, functions, flows,
material ideas and technical parameters; Engineering Canvas; 2–3 critical engineering
questions; 1–2 engineering checks including a quantitative estimate or calculation;
calculations show formula, assumptions, units, result and meaning; explanation of how
biological inspiration was balanced with technical feasibility; no literal copying of
biological form.

**3.3 Evaluation & Iteration** — the two variants compared against the Section-2 KPIs in
a transparent comparison table / decision matrix with justified ratings; engineering
checks used as evidence; peer review, instructor feedback and/or AI as critical reviewer,
assessed critically; selection of Concept A, B or a justified hybrid; revised Final
Concept V2 with explicit what-changed-why-and-on-what-evidence; remaining uncertainties
and next tests. Required chain: **Concept V1a/b → Engineering Review → Evidence → V2**.

**4 Sustainability & Life's Principles** — the Life's Principles most relevant to the
final concept and how well the concept actually applies them; environmental benefits plus
limitations and trade-offs; no unsupported claims such as "sustainable" or "resource
efficient"; material use, durability, repair, reuse, modularity, recyclability,
Cradle-to-Cradle / circular economy where relevant; whether sustainability considerations
changed the design.

**5 Discussion & Conclusion** — whether and how biomimicry produced ideas a conventional
design approach probably would not have produced; critical reflection on strongest and
weakest aspects; whether the original challenge and the performance criteria were
addressed; innovative character and the limits of the available evidence.

**6 References** — varied high-quality sources: scientific papers, AskNature entries,
credible technical sources.

**Appendix A** — AI use statement (table). **Appendix B** — optional supporting material.
