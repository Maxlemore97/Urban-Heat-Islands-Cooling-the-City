# Structure & Content checklist

Verbatim from the course template, broken into atomic items. Each is scored
`PRESENT` / `PARTIAL` / `MISSING` with a page or `file:line` as evidence.

`PARTIAL` means the element exists but does not do what the bullet asks — a decision
matrix with empty cells, a "figure" that is a placeholder, a Life's Principle named
but not assessed. A `[TODO:]` marker is never `PRESENT`.

## Headings (must be used verbatim, not renamed)

`H1` Introduction · `H2` Challenge & Scoping · `H3` Bio-Inspired Design Process with
3.1 Discovering & Abstracting, 3.2 Creating: Engineering Concept Development, 3.3
Concept Evaluation & Iteration · `H4` Sustainability & Life's Principles ·
`H5` Discussion & Conclusion · `H6` References · `H7` Appendix A – AI Use Statement ·
`H8` Appendix B – Optional Supporting Material

## 1 Introduction

- `I1` Background, relevance and aim of the report stated clearly.
- `I2` Design challenge and the intended contribution of the project introduced briefly.

## 2 Challenge & Scoping

- `S1` Problem to be solved defined precisely.
- `S2` A clear design question formulated.
- `S3` Context conditions and constraints under which the solution must work.
- `S4` System of interest described.
- `S5` Relevant subsystems, supersystem and system boundaries described.
- `S6` Key functions identified, **written as verbs**, aligned with the taxonomy explainer.
- `S7` A first abstract functional diagram: main functions and, where relevant, energy,
  material and information flows. **Solution-neutral** — no components, no materials.
- `S8` Biologized questions that guide the search for natural models.
- `S9` **4–7** performance criteria / KPIs — count them — **including Life's Principles**.

## 3.1 Discovering & Abstracting

- `D1` AskNature / AskNature Chat, scientific literature and other credible sources used.
- `D2` Research path explicit: how models were found, which were selected, and **why
  others were de-selected**.
- `D3` Biological strategies and mechanisms described in depth — **how they work**, not
  only what the organism is.
- `D4` Figures or drawings support the explanation.
- `D5` Abstraction into a general transferable principle, with biological observation,
  abstraction and possible technical interpretation **clearly distinguished**.
- `D6` Relevant Life's Principles evident in the selected biological strategies identified.

## 3.2 Creating: Engineering Concept Development

- `C1` Structured ideation method used (Crazy 8s and/or Morphological Matrix).
- `C2` **At least three** initial concept combinations developed before selecting two.
- `C3` **At least two technically distinct** concept variants.
- `C4` Annotated concept drawings showing components, functions, flows, material ideas
  and important technical parameters.
- `C5` An Engineering Canvas.
- `C6` **2–3 critical engineering questions** derived from the concept — assumptions that
  could change the design decision.
- `C7` **1–2 engineering checks** carried out, including a quantitative estimate or
  calculation where reasonably possible.
- `C8` Calculations show formula, assumptions, units, result **and what the result means
  for the concept**.
- `C9` Explanation of how concepts were generated and how biological inspiration was
  balanced with technical feasibility; no literal copying of biological form.

## 3.3 Concept Evaluation & Iteration

- `E1` The two variants compared against the **Section 2** KPIs.
- `E2` A transparent comparison table or decision matrix, with important ratings justified.
- `E3` Engineering checks and critical questions used as evidence in the comparison.
- `E4` Peer review, instructor feedback and/or AI as critical reviewer — and the AI
  feedback **assessed critically by the team**, not merely accepted.
- `E5` Concept A, Concept B or a justified hybrid selected.
- `E6` A revised **Final Concept V2**, with what changed compared with the earlier
  concept and why, explained in detail.
- `E7` Remaining uncertainties, and what should be tested or developed next.

## 4 Sustainability & Life's Principles

- `U1` The Life's Principles most relevant to the final concept, with an **assessment of
  how well the concept actually applies them** — a list is not an assessment.
- `U2` Environmental benefits **and** limitations **and** trade-offs.
- `U3` No unsupported claims such as "sustainable" or "resource efficient".
- `U4` Material use, durability, repair, reuse, modularity, recyclability and/or
  Cradle-to-Cradle / circular economy where relevant.
- `U5` Whether sustainability considerations **led to changes in the design**.

## 5 Discussion & Conclusion

- `R1` Whether and how biomimicry contributed ideas a conventional approach probably
  would not have produced.
- `R2` Critical reflection on the strongest **and weakest** aspects of the final concept.
- `R3` Whether the original challenge and the performance criteria were addressed.
- `R4` Innovative character and the limits of the available evidence.

## 6 References

- `F1` Varied high-quality sources: scientific papers, AskNature entries, credible
  technical sources.
- `F2` Roughly 20 references as a guideline — quality and relevance over count.
- `F3` One consistent citation style throughout.
- `F4` No AI tool cited as a scientific reference for a factual claim.

## Appendices

- `A1` Appendix A – AI Use Statement: which tool, used for what, how it influenced the
  project, how important output was verified. Not a list of prompts.
- `A2` Appendix B – Optional Supporting Material, where useful.

## Formal

- `X1` Body 15–25 pages excluding references and appendices.
- `X2` Template headings used verbatim.
- `X3` All chapters addressed.
- `X4` Every source used is referenced, AI tools included.
