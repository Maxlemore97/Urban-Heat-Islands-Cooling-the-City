\subsection{Background and relevance}

\todo{2--3 paragraphs. Suggested content, to be written out and referenced:}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Urban heat islands (UHI): built-up areas are warmer than their rural
        surroundings, mainly because impervious surfaces absorb short-wave
        radiation, store the heat and release it at night, while
        evapotranspiration and long-wave loss to the sky are reduced
        \parencite{oke1982}.
  \item Magnitude in the reference city: Zurich reports an urban heat island
        of \qtyrange{1}{2}{\kelvin} by day and up to \qty{7}{\kelvin} at night
        in the centre relative to the surrounding area, and sealed sun-exposed
        surfaces exceeding \qty{60}{\celsius} in summer
        \parencite{stadtzuerich_merkblatt}. The night-time load is the larger
        share, and it is the share that impairs sleep and recovery.
  \item Health relevance: the city names cerebrovascular, cardiovascular and
        respiratory illness during heat periods, with older people and small
        children particularly at risk \parencite{stadtzuerich_merkblatt}.
        Mechanical cooling would raise both electricity demand and the waste
        heat released into the street canyon, which is why the city advises
        against air conditioning \parencite[132--133]{stadtzuerich2020}.
  \item Policy context: Zurich's \emph{Fachplanung Hitzeminderung}
        \parencite{stadtzuerich2020} and its implementation agenda
        \parencite{stadtzuerich_agenda} make retrofit measures on existing
        building envelopes an actively pursued field of application --- and
        define the incumbent solution this project has to improve on
        (Section~\ref{sec:stateofart}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Aim of the report and intended contribution}

\todo{State the aim in 3--5 sentences, e.g.:}
This report documents a biomimicry design process for reducing heat load on the
envelope of existing urban buildings. It follows the Biomimicry Design Spiral
\parencite{baumeister2014} through scoping, biological discovery and
abstraction, engineering concept development, validation and evaluation. The
intended contribution is a technically specified, quantitatively checked
envelope concept, together with a transparent account of which biological
mechanisms were transferred, which were rejected, and on what evidence.
