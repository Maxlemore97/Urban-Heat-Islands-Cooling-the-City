# Challenge 1 — Cooling the City, in brief

## Challenge

Zurich's sealed surfaces absorb the summer day and give it back into the street after
sunset, which is why the city centre runs 1–2 K warmer than its surroundings by day but up
to 7 K warmer at night, with sun-exposed surfaces above 60 °C. The design question we cut
from that is: **how might we let the heat that Zurich's existing buildings absorb during a
summer day drive its own removal, so that the hotter it gets, the more the building cools
itself?**

## System of interest

The heat-regulating outer layer of an existing building — one system, not two, because
"prevent heat" and "remove heat" turned out to be two functions of the same thing, coupled
by the design question. Inside the boundary: passive control of the radiation balance at
that surface, temporary storage, a rainwater path, and the self-regulating link between load
and cooling power; outside it: the building behind the layer, anything actively controlled,
seasonal storage and electricity generation, and district-scale greening.

## Functions

The main function is to regulate heat gain and release at an urban surface, split into
prevention during the day (reflect, shade), removal into the night sky and the passing air
(emit, convect, store), and a water path that captures, retains and meters out rainwater.
The one that defines the project is **F9, couple response strength to load** — the cooling
must get stronger as the surface gets hotter, without a controller, a sensor or a pump —
while keeping clean, resisting weathering and attaching to the existing wall are supporting
functions rather than sources of concept variants.
