# Discovering Nature’s Functions and Strategies and Abstracting them

## 1. Elephant Ears

### 1. Biological Example

**African elephant (_Loxodonta africana_)**

Function: Dissipates excess body heat in hot environments. Large ears are especially useful in warm, exposed habitats. ([asknature.org](https://asknature.org/strategy/large-ears-aid-cooling/ "Large Ears Aid Cooling — Biological Strategy — AskNature"))

### 2. Interesting Mechanisms

Warm blood is transported from the body core into the large, thin ears. Their large surface area allows heat to escape into the air. Flapping increases airflow and improves cooling. ([asknature.org](https://asknature.org/strategy/large-ears-aid-cooling/ "Large Ears Aid Cooling — Biological Strategy — AskNature"))

### 3. Abstracting

**Transfer heat to a large surface and increase airflow around it to dissipate heat efficiently.**

## 2. Micro-Finned Surface

### 1. Biological Example

**African elephant (_Loxodonta africana_)**

Function: Sparse body hairs can support heat transfer from the skin. This is relevant for surfaces exposed to warm air and low wind speeds. ([University of Notre Dame](https://sites.nd.edu/biomechanics-in-the-wild/2021/04/06/how-the-largest-land-animal-stays-cool-and-what-we-can-learn-from-it/ "How the Largest Land Animal Stays Cool – And What We Can Learn From It — University of Notre Dame"))

### 2. Interesting Mechanisms

Small structures extending away from a warm surface reach beyond the slow-moving boundary layer close to it. This increases contact with moving air and improves convective heat transfer. AskNature describes the importance of boundary layers for passive cooling. ([asknature.org](https://asknature.org/strategy/small-leaves-buffer-insect-eggs-from-heat/ "Small Leaves Buffer Insect Eggs From Heat — AskNature")) ([University of Notre Dame](https://sites.nd.edu/biomechanics-in-the-wild/2021/04/06/how-the-largest-land-animal-stays-cool-and-what-we-can-learn-from-it/ "How the Largest Land Animal Stays Cool – And What We Can Learn From It — University of Notre Dame"))

### 3. Abstracting

**Add small fins, hairs, or protrusions to a surface to disturb the boundary layer and transfer heat more effectively to passing air.**

## 3. Tree Bark

### 1. Biological Example

**Tree bark**, for example paperbark maple (_Acer griseum_)

Function: Keeps the tree surface cooler under strong sunlight. This is relevant to buildings and urban surfaces exposed to solar radiation. ([asknature.org](https://asknature.org/strategy/bark-keeps-surface-cool-under-the-sun/ "Bark Keeps Surface Cool Under the Sun — Biological Strategy — AskNature"))

### 2. Interesting Mechanisms

The bark reflects part of the incoming solar radiation while efficiently emitting thermal radiation. Rough and layered bark can also create shade, trap insulating air, and improve convection. ([asknature.org](https://asknature.org/strategy/bark-keeps-surface-cool-under-the-sun/ "Bark Keeps Surface Cool Under the Sun — Biological Strategy — AskNature"))

### 3. Abstracting

**Use surfaces that reflect incoming solar energy, emit stored heat, and use texture to create shade and airflow.**

## 4. Cactus Ribs

### 1. Biological Example

**Ribbed cacti, including barrel cacti**

Function: Reduce overheating and water loss in hot, dry environments. This is relevant for exposed building façades and urban surfaces. ([asknature.org](https://asknature.org/strategy/shape-shades-and-enhances-heat-radiation/ "Cactus Ribs Keep Them Cool — Biological Strategy — AskNature"))

### 2. Interesting Mechanisms

The ribs create peaks and troughs. Peaks shade parts of the surface, while the folds increase surface area and disturb airflow. Cooler air can collect in shaded areas and remove heat as it moves upward. ([asknature.org](https://asknature.org/strategy/shape-shades-and-enhances-heat-radiation/ "Cactus Ribs Keep Them Cool — Biological Strategy — AskNature"))

### 3. Abstracting

**Use folded or ribbed surfaces to create self-shading, increase cooling surface area, and improve airflow.**
