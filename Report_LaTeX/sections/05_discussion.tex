\section{Discussion \& Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Contribution of the biomimicry process}
\label{sec:contribution_process}

Biomimicry helped us explore cooling functions before committing to familiar building
solutions. Studying how organisms manage heat encouraged us to abstract and combine
strategies, leading to the proposed link between façade shading, fluid transport and a
roof emitter (Sections~\ref{sec:discovering}--\ref{sec:creating}). Without this process,
we would probably have focused on improving existing solutions individually. Conventional
engineering could produce a similar concept, but the biological models broadened our
search and helped us connect these functions.

\subsection{Strengths and weaknesses of the final concept}
\label{sec:strengths}

V2 offers an unconventional approach for new buildings and especially retrofits: shading
is intended to limit façade heating, while the water loop transfers collected heat to the
roof. This aims to reduce heat release at street level without relying on reflective
façades that could increase exposure for pedestrians and neighboring buildings. Modular
panels could be installed where cooling is most needed. Heat-driven circulation and a
closed loop offer the prospect of operation without continuous pumping or intentional
evaporative water use. However, circulation and the available roof area are the main
limitations. The calculated pipe resistance exceeds the available driving pressure,
requiring a revised network with shorter parallel circuits (Section~\ref{sec:v2check}).
Pump-free circulation remains unverified, and refilling may require a pump. Testing must
establish whether the revised system delivers the intended cooling.

\subsection{Assessment of the challenge and the KPIs}
\label{sec:assessment}

The concept addresses the façade's contribution to urban heat; it does not address all
causes of the wider challenge. The KPIs guided design decisions: heat-flow estimates
limited the connected façade area, and the water constraint favored a loop without
intentional evaporation (Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}). They have therefore supported
concept development, but target achievement has not been demonstrated. Reflectance,
shading duration, durability and actual cooling performance still need verification.
Winter operation, glare and heritage compatibility also remain open. These gaps define
the next evaluation steps.

\subsection{Innovative character and limits of the evidence}
\label{sec:innovation}

The innovative aspect is the combination of biological principles in a maintainable
façade system intended for new buildings and retrofits. We do not claim that its
components are new. Our main lesson from the course is that biological ideas become more
useful when calculations and practical constraints shape their technical transfer. The
estimates support further development, but do not establish measured cooling performance.
Circulation and roof-output tests are the priorities for assessing the concept's
potential.
