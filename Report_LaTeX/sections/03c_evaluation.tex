Reduced air exchange in Modellierungsgebiet~03 makes \textbf{B}'s airflow-dependent
benefit uncertain \cite{stadtzuerich_web}, \cite[pp.~148--151]{stadtzuerich2020},
\cite{blocken2009}. Elephant hairs can aid heat transfer at low wind, whereas transverse
wooden ribs reduced natural convection in another study \cite{myhrvold2012,ahmed2024}.
These contrasting results support testing the coating rather than assigning it a cooling
value.

\textbf{A} was selected because it combines shading with heat transport to a separate
release surface. Its greater material and installation needs remain a trade-off. The
review in Table~\ref{tab:review} guides the V2 construction changes and the revised
engineering assessment below.

\subsubsection{Final Concept V2 --- Construction and Heat Paths}
\label{sec:v2}

V2 moves the pipes outside the original wall, allowing both new construction and
retrofit. The wall is retained apart from mounting connections. A small gap behind the
added layer is intended to let escaped water fall away externally, reducing hidden
wall-damage risk without guaranteeing leak-proof protection
(Figure~\ref{fig:v2sections}).

Heat passes from the façade panel into water, then from the roof pipes into the carrier
plate and coated surface (Figure~\ref{fig:v2loop}). Good pipe-to-panel contact is
essential. The Saharan silver ant inspires solar reflection and infrared emission
\cite{shi2015}. The assumed 95\,\% reflectance still needs material verification. No
increase in convection is assumed and convective heat release requires a surface warmer
than the air.

\subsubsection{Pedestrian Space and Maintenance Access}
\label{sec:v2access}

Compared with V1, we cut back the timber fins to start at the first floor, about 3.4\,m
above ground (Figure~\ref{fig:v2fins}). A new building could set its wall back to
accommodate the fins. This retrofit must retain the wall beside the pavement. Reducing
the modelled ground-floor projection from 0.66\,m to 0.20\,m leaves 0.46\,m more space.
Door operation and remaining pavement width still need checking.

summer shade with winter sunlight. Timber treatment, coating durability, chemical safety
and glare remain material checks. Reflective roofs may increase winter heating demand
\cite{epa_coolroofs}.

\subsubsection{Updated Engineering Assessment}
\label{sec:v2check}

For an illustrative roof output of 60\,W/m\textsuperscript{2}, the 740\,m\textsuperscript{2}
cooling area releases about 44\,kW, supporting roughly 440\,m\textsuperscript{2} of façade
at 100\,W/m\textsuperscript{2}. Repeating the circulation check with the same height and
common route gives Table~\ref{tab:pipesize2}.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering\small
\caption{Pipe-size estimates after reducing the connected heat load.}
\label{tab:pipesize2}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
\begin{tabular}{l l l l}
\toprule
\textbf{Water difference} & \textbf{Buoyancy pressure} & \textbf{Water flow at 44\,kW} & \textbf{Internal diameter} \\
\midrule
10\,°C (= 10\,K) & $\approx$300\,Pa & $\approx$1.1\,kg/s & $\approx$120\,mm \\
3\,°C (= 3\,K) & $\approx$90\,Pa & $\approx$3.5\,kg/s & $\approx$255\,mm \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The drawn roof uses four long coils. Area divided by 0.16\,m pipe spacing gives about
$740/0.16 \approx 4{,}620$\,m of pipe, or 1,150\,m per coil. Assuming 40\,mm internal
diameter, equal flow and a 10\,°C water difference, the Darcy equation gives about
20\,kPa loss per coil at 44\,kW total load, excluding bends. This exceeds the 0.30\,kPa
buoyancy pressure by roughly a factor of 67: the long-coil arrangement must change.

The roof and water-flow estimates prescribe temperatures separately. Water temperature,
flow and pipe-to-surface heat transfer must be solved together before assigning an
operating output. The rendered long coils therefore remain a layout to revise, with
short parallel circuits and larger connecting pipes.

Roof-pipe water volume is approximately
$4{,}620\ \text{m} \times \pi \times (0.04\ \text{m})^2 / 4 \approx 5.8$\,m\textsuperscript{3},
excluding façade pipes and headers. This makes roof loading and winter water storage
important checks.

\runin{Winter operation.}
\label{sec:winter}
Before frost, the proposed circuit is emptied into storage and refilled when needed.
Drainback requires sloping pipes and air entry, Baechler and Love
\cite[Ch.~3]{baechler2007} specify a slope of about 2.1\,\%. Trapped water and pump-assisted
refill remain checks.
