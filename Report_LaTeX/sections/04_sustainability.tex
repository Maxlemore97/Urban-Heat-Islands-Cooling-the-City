\section{Sustainability \& Life's Principles}
\label{sec:sustainability}

\subsection{Life's Principles applied to Concept V2}
\label{sec:lp_applied}

\subsubsection{Be resource efficient --- minimize the use of external resources}
\label{sec:lp_resource}

Cooling does not need auxiliary electricity.
The water circulation is driven by buoyancy alone and the loop is closed (Section~\ref{sec:check1}).
Therefore no additional water is consumed in the operation.

\subsubsection{Adapt to changing conditions --- day/night and seasonal cycles}
\label{sec:lp_adapt}

The daily cycle is used by the system.
The water flow automatically adjusts to the given temperature.
The seasonal cycle needs to be addressed by draining the pipe system (Section~\ref{sec:v2check}).

\subsubsection{Use life-friendly chemistry --- no toxic chemicals or materials}
\label{sec:lp_chemistry}
The usage of water instead of anti-freeze liquids such as glycol speaks the most for the life-friendly chemistry principle.
Instead of finding life-friendly alternatives, the project leaves out toxic chemicals in general.

\subsection{Environmental benefits, limitations and trade-offs}
\label{sec:benefits}

Concept V2 benefits from a cooling system without the use of auxiliary electricity reducing the need for conventional cooling methods such as air conditioning.
By releasing the excess thermal energy into the sky the concept reduces the urban heat island effect in the street canyons (Section~\ref{sec:check2}).
An additional upside of the concept V2 compared to V1 is the ability to retrofit an existing building.

The main trade off with concept V2 is the alteration of the façade.
The construction also adds an additional layer over the existing façade making the building reach further into the street (Section~\ref{sec:v2access}).

\subsection{Materials, durability, repair and circularity}
\label{sec:materials}

V2 retains the existing wall and roof but adds timber ribs, panels, pipework and a coated roof surface.
Removable façade panels provide access to the pipes for inspection and repair without opening the wall (Sections~\ref{sec:v2}--\ref{sec:v2access}).
This supports the repair of individual modules and allows for a modular installation, where modules can be installed only in particularly exposed areas instead of covering an entire wall.

The modular design of the modules themselves should also make recycling, or at least disassembly into individual parts, easier without requiring complex separation processes.
This could allow components such as the pipes or timber shades to be reused.

\clearpage

\subsection{Did sustainability considerations change the design?}
\label{sec:designchanges}

From the beginning, we wanted a closed water loop to avoid wasting fresh water and passive cooling without external energy to drive circulation or other mechanisms.
Sustainability did not lead to further design changes because it had been a key factor from the start.

The only change during the process that also had an impact on sustainability was the modularization of the modules.
It makes repair and installation easier and reduces the amount of material that needs to be replaced during repairs.
However, this was more of a side effect than a conscious sustainability decision.
