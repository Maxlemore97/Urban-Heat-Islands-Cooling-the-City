# Introduction lecture — course mechanics and framing

Source: `1 Introduction.pptx` (Musiolik / Baumgartner), 28 slides.

## Assessment — the numbers

| Component | Weight | Form |
|---|---|---|
| **Group report** | **70 %** | one per team, uploaded to Moodle, filename = team name/number + challenge |
| **Moodle test** | **30 %** | individual, in class at the end of the course, digital, 30 min, MC **and open questions**, **all lectures are covered** |

Further rules:
- Teams of **4 students**, interdisciplinary; template and grading categories on Moodle.
- Attendance obligatory; only excused absences of at most one day.
- **English**: no separate mark, but **up to 20 % deduction** if the assignment is hard to
  follow because of language. This is why the academic-writing handout
  ([`../overview/04_report_requirements.md`](../overview/04_report_requirements.md)) is
  worth twenty minutes.
- Coaching is optional: one 15-minute slot per team, booked on Moodle, with a **specific
  question prepared beforehand**.

The Moodle test detail matters for how we use these notes: *all lectures* are examinable,
including the ones with no bearing on our own challenge.

## Framing (slides 17–23)

Benyus' definition, used in the course:

> **"Biomimicry is innovation inspired by nature."** — and the operative question:
> *"What in the natural world has already solved what I'm trying to solve?"*

Life's Principles as named in the film (slide 20) — the informal version of the six:
life runs on sunlight · does its chemistry in water · **depends on the local** · banks on
diversity · wastes nothing · uses safe elements.

Slide 23 lists the problem classes biomimicry addresses. Four of the six are ours:

| Class | Example given |
|---|---|
| Energy efficiency | shark skin reducing drag |
| Materials science | spider silk |
| Structural design | honeycomb |
| **Self-cleaning** | **lotus effect** → our F10 |
| **Water and moisture management** | **fog-drinking beetle** → our F6 |
| **Climate regulation and ventilation** | **termite mounds, "passively air-conditioned buildings that do not require air conditioning"** → our F0, and the model we de-select on evidence grounds |

That last row is worth knowing: the termite mound is the course's own headline example, and
we reject it (Turner 2008). Rejecting the lecturer's stock example **with a citation** is a
good move, but it needs to be done politely and on evidence — which is how Section 2
already words it.
