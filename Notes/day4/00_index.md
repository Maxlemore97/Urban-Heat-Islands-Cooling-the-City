## The three things to act on

1. **Our concept is currently framed as *less bad*.** The C2C distinction is the sharpest
   lever we have in Section 4 — see 01, section "Eco-efficiency against eco-effectiveness".
2. **Circular does not mean sustainable.** The lecture's own counter-example is a plastic
   additive that is harmless in a laptop case and hazardous in a toy after recycling. That
   is our biocide argument, arriving from the material side.
3. **The wear rule decides which layer belongs in which cycle** — and it has a clean answer
   for our build-up. See 01.
