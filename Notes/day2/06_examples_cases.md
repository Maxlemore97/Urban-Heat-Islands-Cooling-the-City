# Example PDFs and cases — what they are worth

## The five example clippings

Handed out as illustrations of "comb the literature", all New Scientist 2018. None is
thermally relevant; do not force them into our model table.

| File | Content | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `Example 1 Natural born metals.pdf` | Mantis shrimp club, nacre; nanoscale arrangement gives strength **and** toughness, which normally trade off | Good illustration of "structure, not material, delivers the property" — the same argument we make for a diffuse white surface vs a mirror |
| `Example 2 Dragonfly wings.pdf` | Emerging dragonflies excrete sodium bicarbonate that reacts with water in the wings to form CO₂ pores; copied to make **aerogel** at ~$4/kg | The one with a thermal edge: aerogels are insulation. Relevant only if a concept needs a low-conductivity layer |
| `Example 3 Artificial muscles - made of wood.pdf` | Delignified wood + hydrogel; 80× stronger than skeletal muscle | Method example of abstraction from structure |
| `Example 4 Luminous Fireflies.pdf` | Bioluminescence doubles as a warning signal to bats | Illustrates multi-functionality (one signal, two functions) |
| `Example tree frog.pdf` | Title slide only, "How does nature attach to wet surfaces?" | No content |

## `Example Interface - Biomimicry Design.pdf`

Interface's carpet-tile case (36 p., marketing brochure). The useful part is the abstraction
logic: forest-floor variability → carpet tiles that need not match, so tiles can be replaced
individually and installation waste drops. Also features SMIT's "Ivy League" — photovoltaic
elements applied to vertical surfaces, inspired by ivy growth patterns and photosynthesis.

Worth one line as a precedent for **modularity on a vertical surface** (our F12 and the
"built to shape" checklist item). Not a technical source.

## `Biomimicry - Inspiration reader.pdf`

24 pages of assorted New Scientist clippings (honeycomb rock formations, self-driving car
ethics, meerkat sentries, rewilding, bioinspired devices). Broad inspiration, essentially no
thermal content. Low priority for us.
