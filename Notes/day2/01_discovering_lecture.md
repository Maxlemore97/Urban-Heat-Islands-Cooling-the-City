## 4 DISCOVER — search broadly, then compare (slide 6)

> **"Search by function: regulate temperature, move air, exchange gases — not 'termite
> building'."**

Sources named: AskNature / AskNature Chat · scientific papers · biology books and
databases · field observations.

**For each model the lecture wants six fields documented** — this is the column set our
model table in Section 3.1 should have:

| organism/system | function | structure/process | mechanism | conditions | evidence/source |

The lecture's own example candidates (slide 6) overlap our challenge almost completely:
termite nests → ventilation · cactus ribs → shading / heat exchange · elephant ears → heat
dissipation · fur / feathers → insulation.

## Biologizing the question (slides 10–13)

Two forms:
- **How would nature [function]?**
- **How would nature [function] given [context constraints]?**

Worked examples given (slide 11) — the second is ours almost verbatim:

| Design question | Biologized question |
|---|---|
| How might we reduce stormwater runoff and flooding in cities? | How does nature manage excess water? |
| **How might we keep buildings cool in the summer?** | **How does nature regulate temperature in hot climates?** |
| How might we reduce the use of toxic substances in paints? | How does nature create color? |

**The inverse question (slide 13)** — worth one line in our report:

> "The answers to *'What would nature NOT do?'* can often be as informative to the design
> process as what nature would do."

For us the inverse answers are sharp: nature would not use a mirror finish, would not run
a compressor, would not pump potable water onto a wall, and would not build a single
fixed-response surface for both January and July.

## The four ways to find models (slides 14–29)

1. **Go outside and observe** — find organisms doing what you want to do; note the
   *conditions* that influence the strategy. (The outdoor exercise, note 07.)
2. **Brainstorm with biologists** — university, natural history museum, zoo; a "Biologist
   at the Design Table" (BaDT); AskNature, built by biologists on the Taxonomy.
3. **Comb the literature (slide 18)** — three prompts worth using literally:
   - *"Whose survival depends on doing what I want to do?"* → champion adapters
   - *"Find the truly challenged"* — organisms most exposed to our problem yet unfazed by
     it. For us: what lives on a sun-baked south-facing rock face all summer?
   - *"Explore the unexpected"* — organisms that should not thrive in these conditions.
4. **Apply different lenses (slides 24–29)**:

| Lens | Use it when | Our reading |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| **Function** | searching for a need to solve | F1–F9 as verbs → "How would nature reflect / emit / store / meter?" |
| **Ecological** | designing a system of interacting parts | the street canyon as an ecosystem; our layer as one member of it |
| **Naturalist** | sourcing ideas, staying open | the outdoor exercise |
| **Operating condition** | a specific environmental condition must be managed | **our strongest lens** — intense solar radiation, 60 °C surfaces, freeze–thaw, weeks without rain |
| **Local** | the design stays in one geographic location | **also ours** — the design never leaves Zurich, 47.38° N, temperate broadleaf forest biome (see note 05) |

The lecture explicitly asks: *"What do you see in your project using the different
lenses?"* — answering that per lens is a cheap way to make the research path explicit,
which is the part of criterion 2 most teams lose points on.

## 5 ABSTRACT — the crucial bridge (slides 33–34)

> **"Do not stop at the organism. The mechanism is more important than the name."**

The five-field abstraction, with the lecture's termite example:

| Organism | Structure | Process | Function | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Termite colony | porous nest + channels | pressure / buoyancy driven airflow | exchange heat and gases | *what is actually supported by sources?* |

And the three-column separation we must keep strict (slide 33):

| BIOLOGY | ABSTRACTION | DESIGN PRINCIPLE |
|---|---|---|
| Connected openings and internal pathways support exchange of air and gases under changing environmental conditions. | Use distributed openings, connected channels and pressure/temperature differences to drive passive exchange. | Create controlled passive airflow through geometry and pressure differences — **without copying the mound shape**. |

Note that our Section 2 already de-selects the termite mound on evidence grounds
(Turner 2008), which is exactly the "what is actually supported by sources?" column doing
its job. That is worth saying out loud in Section 3.1.

## AI rule (slide 31)

> "AskNature Chat or ChatGPT can help you find and explain candidates. **AI output is not
> evidence — verify important biological claims.**"

Every AI-surfaced candidate needs an AskNature profile or a peer-reviewed paper behind it
before it enters the model table, and the check belongs in Appendix A.
