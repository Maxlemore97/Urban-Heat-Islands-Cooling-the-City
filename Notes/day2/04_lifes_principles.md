# Life's Principles — lecture and checklist

Sources: `2 Scoping - Lifes principles 01092026.pdf` (34 slides) and
`Lifes Principles Checklist.pdf` (Biomimicry 3.8 Resource Handbook).

**Applied to our project → `../../Working documents/lifes_principles_options.md`.** This note is the raw
material.

## What they are (slides 4–6)

> "Life's principles represent the overarching patterns found amongst species surviving and
> thriving on earth." — they are *aspirational ideals* and **sustainable benchmarks we can
> use to measure whether our designs are accomplishing these principles**.

## How to use them (slides 29, 33)

- Integrate from the very beginning; **define them as KPIs**.
- Choose **only relevant** principles — "use them to guide, not decorate, the design".
- **Make them testable.** Instead of "resource efficient", ask: *can we achieve the target
  with less material / energy than the reference solution?*
- Measure with the checklist as a sustainability benchmark.
- The lecture's own example of a relevant set is **passive cooling**: exploit local wind /
  temperature differences · minimise operating energy · avoid unnecessary material · adapt
  to changing external conditions.

## The six principles, with the lecture's examples

| Principle | Sub-principles named | Examples given |
|---|---|---|
| **Evolve to survive** | replicate strategies that work · integrate the unexpected · reshuffle information | corvids teaching tool use; the bicycle since 1817; **Post-it from a failed glue**; employees changing jobs |
| **Be resource-efficient (material and energy)** | multi-functional design · fit form to function · **use low energy processes** | duck preening = five functions in one strategy; mobile phone; **hollow bird bones**; **Trombe wall** — absorbs solar heat by day, releases it at night by thermal diffusion, removing the need for mechanical systems |
| **Adapt to changing conditions** | incorporate diversity · embody resilience through variation, redundancy, decentralization · maintain integrity through self-renewal | **Arctic fox and ptarmigan change coat colour with the season**; Blockbuster vs Netflix; **self-healing concrete with resin microcapsules** |
| **Integrate development with growth** | self-organize · build from the bottom up | mangrove islands; open-source software, Wikipedia |
| **Be locally attuned and responsive** | use readily available materials and energy | **Namib desert beetle** harvesting fog with hydrophobic/hydrophilic patterning; **photochromic lenses** that darken under UV and revert without it |
| **Use life-friendly chemistry** | build selectively with a small subset of elements · do chemistry in water | blue mussel glue curing underwater; **Bayer replacing 50–90 % of petrochemical solvents with water** in polyurethane coatings |

## The checklist, in full

### CREATE CONDITIONS CONDUCIVE TO LIFE
- all aspects and elements of the design are **optimized rather than maximized**
- the design leverages its interdependence in the system
- the design enhances the system's capacity to support life over the long haul

### EVOLVE TO SURVIVE
- success is based on whether it contributes to the continuity of life
- builds on what works
- opportunities for cross-pollination of information and ideas
- receives and incorporates an influx of new information
- mistakes are used to encourage continual idea generation

### INTEGRATE DEVELOPMENT WITH GROWTH
- both development and growth are optimized
- components are modular and nested
- **built to shape** (no cutting, therefore no waste cuttings)
- components of the system are self-organizing
- fosters emergent relationships
- creates more opportunities (niches) for life

### ADAPT TO CHANGING CONDITIONS
- adapts to **temporal and spatial changes**
- maintains integrity by constantly adding energy, information, matter to improve the system
- **withstands disturbance while maintaining function**
- incorporates a variety of different forms
- **duplicates critical elements**
- functions are distributed and decentralized
- multiple forms, processes and systems meet functional needs over time and space
- co-evolves with other parts of the system to increase the rate of adaptation

### BE LOCALLY ATTUNED AND RESPONSIVE
- fits into and integrates with the surrounding environment
- is resourceful with opportunities and limitations
- **materials are local and abundant**
- **harnesses freely available energy**
- fosters symbiotic, cooperative, community-savvy relationships
- **avoids competition by finding a new niche**
- cooperates with other parts of the system to make the most of what is available
- maintains community ties through reciprocal actions
- co-evolves with other parts of the system
- **leverages existing cycles**
- **detects local feedback loops**
- **responds quickly and appropriately to feedback**
- learns by trial, error and imitation
- **the signal it sends, the "antennae" it uses to detect the signal, and its response match**

### USE LIFE-FRIENDLY CHEMISTRY
- uses chemistry that supports life processes
- assembles a small subset of elements in elegant ways to achieve function
- uses a safe subset of the periodic table
- **the breakdown of the design results only in by-products that cause no harm**
- all chemistry is done using water as the solvent
- chemical reactions tap the power of self-assembly

### BE RESOURCE EFFICIENT (MATERIAL AND ENERGY)
- skillfully and conservatively takes advantage of resources and opportunities
- is respectful of the limits of habitat (water, nutrients, niches)
- **meets its functional needs with minimal outlay of material and energy**
- **a single design meets more than one need**
- takes the energy path of least resistance
- reduces required temperatures, pressures and/or time for reactions
- reuses materials or uses recycled materials
- stays in either a technical or a biological cascade
- **is designed for disassembly, reuse and reconfiguration**
- **uses shape and information to reduce the use of material and energy**
- the form provides the function
- **taps the power of limits**
