# Challenge brief — the instructors' own wording

Source: `Proposals of projects  challenges - handout.docx`, "17 Engineering Challenges for
Bio-Inspired Innovation", section A "Urban Systems and Architecture".

## Challenge 1 — Urban Heat Islands: Cooling the City

> "Cities are facing increasing heat stress due to climate change. **Develop bioinspired
> strategies to reduce urban heat through building materials, façade design, or urban
> planning.**"

**Guiding questions given with the challenge:**

1. How does nature **reflect, absorb, or release heat** efficiently?
2. How do organisms **regulate temperature in hot environments**?
3. What can we learn from **desert plants or animals** about passive cooling?

These are pre-biologized questions from the instructors. Our own set in Section 2.6 should be
checked against them: we cover (1) and (2) well; **(3) is the one we answer only implicitly**
— our model table contains desert organisms (Saharan silver ant, *Encelia farinosa*) but we
never say that the desert is where the champion adapters for this challenge live. One
sentence fixes that, and it also justifies the model selection.

Note that the challenge names three scales — **materials, façade design, urban planning** —
and our Section 2 explicitly cuts to the first two. That cut should be visible as a decision,
which it is.

## Neighbouring challenges worth knowing

Three of the other sixteen overlap ours; if the world café puts us next to one of these
teams, this is the shared ground:

- **2 · Water Scarcity — Collecting and Storing Water.** "Inspired by organisms that collect,
  transport, store, and conserve water." Guiding questions: fog and dew collection, storage
  in cacti and succulents, **"How does nature prevent water loss through evaporation?"** Our
  F6–F8 are their whole challenge.
- **3 · Energy-Efficient Buildings — Nature's Climate Control.** Climate-adaptive building
  concept reducing heating, cooling and ventilation demand. **"How could several passive
  strategies be combined in one building?"**
- **4 · Building Insulation Inspired by Nature.** Fur, feathers, air pockets, layering. This
  is the winter half of our summer/winter trade-off.
- **10 · Energy-Efficient Data Centers** also asks "How does nature dissipate and transport
  heat efficiently?" — same biology, different boundary.

The boundary between us and challenge 2 is worth being able to state in one sentence: **they
harvest water as the goal; we harvest water as a means to move heat.**
